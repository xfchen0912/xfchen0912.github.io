Research vision

How do cells change state in diseased tissues?

I study how gene regulation and signals from neighbouring cells shape cellular plasticity. My goal is to identify which programs sustain disease-associated states, and which changes in regulation or tissue context might shift those states.

Research directions

Regulatory programs in transition
I use single-cell gene expression and chromatin accessibility to ask which transcription factors act together as cells change state, and how their influence varies over a transition. Explore scMagnify
Cells in their spatial niches
I build models of spatial transcriptomics to ask why cells of the same type behave differently across a tissue, and which differences are associated with their local neighbours or the broader tissue condition. Explore KALEIDO
Predicting responses to perturbation
I aim to predict how a cell would respond to a change in condition or neighbourhood, and to identify which predictions carry across tissue contexts. Liver disease provides a setting to study these questions alongside hepatocyte plasticity and tissue remodelling. See KALEIDO’s perturbation approach

Method development · Nature Communications

scMagnify

Interpretable deep learning for dynamic gene regulation

scMagnify integrates single-cell gene expression and chromatin accessibility to infer time-lagged gene-regulatory networks. It decomposes these networks into groups of transcription factors whose activity can be tracked along a cell-state transition.

This helps identify candidate regulators of cell-state changes and trace how signals from neighbouring cells may connect to transcriptional responses inside a cell.

Spatial generative modelling

KALEIDO

Select a face of the cube to explore the model.

KALEIDO uses a graph-based generative model to represent cell-intrinsic state, tissue condition and local niche separately in spatial transcriptomics.

The model is designed to ask how the same cell state might respond differently across niches, or how a change in tissue condition could alter expression directly or through the local microenvironment.

Single-cell and spatial multi-omics

Liver disease progression

I use transcriptomic, chromatin accessibility and spatial profiles to study how hepatocyte states and their tissue niches change across metabolic liver disease, fibrosis and liver cancer.

I ask which regulatory changes accompany hepatocyte plasticity, and how fibroblast and immune-cell remodelling may help sustain disease-associated states.
